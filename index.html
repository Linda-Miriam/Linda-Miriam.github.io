INT. The Writer’s Room — Day0101

Extraordinary characters. Untold histories.

Strong, complex women — and stories that refuse to stay in their genre.

Linda Miriam Azamir is a Los Angeles–based screenwriter whose work spans historical drama, adventure, and animation. Her credits include the feature film Jerusalem ’67, Seasons 2 and 3 of Unpacked’s The Jewish Story, and the children’s animated series Koga and Friends, which won first place in the Animation category at the 2022 Omni Cultural TV Fest.

Drawn to extraordinary characters and untold stories, Linda creates character-driven narratives that combine history, adventure, and complex human relationships. Her original feature screenplay Piratical, inspired by the lives of Anne Bonny and Mary Read, reimagines the Golden Age of Piracy through the stories of two women determined to forge their own destinies.

Linda holds a B.A. in English from UC Berkeley, where she studied creative writing.

Produced Feature Film · Historical War Drama · 2026

Jerusalem ’67

Set against the backdrop of the 1967 Six-Day War, Jerusalem ’67 tells the intertwined stories of a courageous civilian woman drawn to the front lines, a father torn between family and duty, and a paratrooper commander thrust into the battle for Jerusalem. Inspired by true events, this sweeping historical drama explores courage, sacrifice, and the human cost of war.

Logline

In 1719, a teenage wife steals Blackbeard’s diary and poses as a male pirate to hunt his lost gold, while her one-eyed husband commandeers a Royal Navy warship to reclaim her.

Nassau, 1719. The Golden Age of Piracy is dying.

Seventeen-year-old ANNE BONNY is trapped in a marriage to a charming, ruthless con man who considers her his property. When her husband, JAMES, obtains the private journal of the dead pirate Blackbeard, Anne realizes she can decipher the clues to a legendary hidden fortune. She steals the journal and escapes with the notorious pirate CALICO JACK RACKHAM, hoping the treasure will buy her freedom.

But escaping a husband is easier than escaping his reach. After a brief capture, Anne disguises herself as a boy named Adam and flees to sea, shooting out James’s eye in the process. Humiliated and obsessed with reclaiming his wife, James begins a relentless pursuit that will transform him into the feared pirate hunter known as Snake Eyes.

Aboard Calico’s ship, Anne finds an unlikely ally in MARY READ, a formidable sailor and former soldier who has spent nearly her entire life disguised as a man. Mary recognizes Anne’s intelligence and determination, and the two form a powerful friendship. While Mary teaches Anne to fight, Anne uses Blackbeard’s journal to guide the crew toward the buried gold. Together, they orchestrate daring deceptions, outwit the Royal Navy, and rescue Blackbeard’s last surviving confidant, the only man who knows where the treasure lies.

But their successes draw danger. James commandeers a Royal Navy warship and hunts them along the Carolina coast, while resentment grows within Calico’s crew toward the young sailor who increasingly drives their expedition. Anne finally deciphers the journal’s greatest mystery: the treasure lies on Roanoke Island, where England’s Lost Colony vanished.

There, betrayal threatens everything. A treacherous crewmate exposes Anne as a woman, stripping her of the authority she has earned. Mary is exposed as well. With their treasure and ship about to be stolen, the two women fight back, defeat the mutineers, and compel the crew to accept them openly as women. No longer hiding behind Calico, Anne leads them to Blackbeard’s gold.

But buried beneath the fortune is something more extraordinary: a deed to unclaimed land where a free port might be established, beyond the reach of the authorities and conventions that have ruled their lives. For Anne, it represents something greater than riches—a place where she can finally live under her own name. Yet the crew wants to spend its gold, and even Calico refuses to follow her dream.

Before Anne can secure their future, another Royal Navy warship attacks. Calico surrenders, but Anne and Mary refuse. In the ensuing battle, Anne confronts James and finally breaks his hold over her, sending him to his death. The two women fight until they are overwhelmed and captured.

Sentenced to hang in Jamaica, both escape immediate execution because they are pregnant. Calico goes to the gallows. Mary dies in prison, leaving Anne the ring that symbolizes the life she once knew beyond her disguise.

Months later, facing transportation back to the world she fled, Anne stages a desperate escape with the help of her loyal friend Pierre. Reunited with Blackbeard’s chart, she sails toward the promised haven.

She began her journey running from men who owned her. Now she sails toward a place where she can stand on her own.
